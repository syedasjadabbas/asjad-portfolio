MockAI

A multimodal interview-evaluation system that scores each recorded answer on content, delivery and composure, and fuses the three into an explainable report. Candidate and admin platforms and the full AI evaluation pipeline are complete and tested end to end.

Role AI Engineer (Final Year Project)
Category AI Systems & Backend Pipelines
Timeline Final Year Project · 2026
Core Stack FastAPI · React · MongoDB · DistilBERT · FERPlus

Problem & Challenge

Interview practice tools usually grade only the text of an answer. A human interviewer also judges how the answer was delivered and how composed the candidate looked, and that feedback normally needs a person to review every recording.

The engineering challenge was to score content, speech delivery and facial composure from a single recorded answer, combine them into one fair score, and keep working when a camera or microphone is missing, without letting one signal inflate the result.

Technical Approach

MockAI has a React candidate platform, a separate admin platform and a FastAPI backend with JWT authentication and role-based access. Each recorded answer is stored and evaluated by a background evaluation worker, so the interview flow never waits on model inference.

Evaluation Pipeline
Recorded answer (WebM)
Captured in the browser and uploaded per question
↓
FFmpeg normalisation
16 kHz mono WAV audio and indexed H.264 MP4 video
↓
Three analyzers
Content: Google Speech-to-Text + DistilBERT embeddings · Delivery: pace, pauses, fillers · Composure: YuNet face detection + FERPlus emotion classification
↓
Late fusion & report
50% content · 30% delivery · 20% composure, difficulty-weighted, saved to MongoDB with an explainable breakdown

Engineering Challenges & Solutions

1. Keeping a silent answer from scoring well

A candidate who says nothing but looks calm could score highly if missing modalities simply had their weight redistributed.

Solution: a verbal-primacy rule. If an answer has no speech, content and delivery score zero and composure stays capped at its 20% weight, so a silent answer cannot score above 20%.

2. Missing camera or microphone

Candidates do not always have both devices available, and failing the evaluation would end the interview.

Solution: graceful degradation with fixed weight tables. Audio only uses 62.5% content and 37.5% delivery; a typed answer uses content only; typed with camera uses 71.4% content and 28.6% composure.

3. Comparing answers of different difficulty

An average of raw question scores treats an easy question and a hard one as equal.

Solution: difficulty-weighted aggregation (easy 1.0×, medium 1.25×, hard 1.5×). Skipped questions score zero but keep their weight, so skipping cannot raise the overall score.

Explainable Feedback

  • Score rationale: each report shows how the content, delivery and composure scores combined into the final score.
  • Strengths and gaps: rubric concepts covered and missed, with STAR-method coaching suggestions.
  • Confidence and stress: a combined reading from acoustic and facial signals, tracked across sessions.

Testing

An end-to-end acceptance run executed all three modalities on a real recorded answer, from FFmpeg normalisation through fused scoring. The repository includes automated tests for media normalisation, speech recognition, the NLP scorer, facial analysis, fusion rules, role-based access and the end-to-end pipeline.
